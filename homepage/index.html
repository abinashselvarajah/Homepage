Computervision

Synthetic vs. Real

Real training images for industrial recognition systems are scarce, so we asked how far synthetic images can take over.

We recreated a real dataset of 3D-modelled Makeblock parts in Unity and generated synthetic variants with the Perception tool, varying colour, rotation, texture, background and lighting to cover what the real photos lack. Classification and detection networks were then trained on different mixes of real and synthetic images and compared.

Synthetic data extends real data well, but only when the randomization is chosen carefully.

Robotics

Autonomous Vehicles and Artificial Intelligence 2023

Driving a racetrack with nothing but a camera and LiDAR: our TurtleBot3 had to find its own way between coloured cones.

The robot detects the cones with a YOLOv5 model and places them in space by fusing the detections with LiDAR distances. Because its odometry drifted, Cartographer SLAM corrected its position, and the cones were collected in an occupancy map. A controller then steers towards the midpoint between the nearest blue and yellow cone.

Autonomous Vehicles and Artificial Intelligence 2025

Same task, faster hardware: we ported the approach to an F1TENTH racecar.

A RealSense camera and a YOLOv11 model detect the cones, trained on real images and on different shares of synthetic ones. The detections are fused with LiDAR data, clustered with DBSCAN and mapped, and the controller aims for the midpoint between the nearest blue and yellow cone, predicting a missing cone when only one colour is visible.

Human-Centered Robotics

A worker with cerebral palsy presses small rubber and metal parts in a sheltered workshop; placing the metal part and operating the lever are the hardest steps. We built a robot assistant for them.

After a workshop visit we defined a persona and requirements and worked in Scrum sprints. The ABB YuMi is programmed in Python with hand-taught motion targets. To pick up loose parts, OpenCV finds each part and its orientation in a webcam image, and a 3D-printed holder keeps the metal part stable. The worker controls the robot by voice, with the hot word “Hey YuMi” and the OpenAI API for small talk, while an emergency stop secures every movement.

Natural Language Processing

Membership Inference Attacks

Was a given text part of a language model’s training data? Membership inference attacks answer this, which matters for copyright and data leakage, but a single attack is unreliable when the data distribution varies.

I therefore combine many attack signals instead of trusting one: I extended an existing framework from 10 to 644 attack features, and replaced its linear combiner with more complex classifiers such as XGBoost and an MLP. The attack is tested on Pythia-2.8B and 6.9B across eight subsets of The Pile.

Blue Team vs. Red Team

Differential privacy is meant to keep training data private. How well does it hold up against membership inference?

We played both sides. As defender, we trained a DistilBERT sentiment classifier on IMDB reviews with DP-SGD at different privacy budgets. As attacker, we used the RMIA and LiRA attacks against it and compared how attack success, accuracy and F1 change with the budget.

LLMs for Repurposing

Repurposing keeps used products in use, but finding a new purpose is hard. Can LLMs help? As a co-author, I contributed to a systematic evaluation of GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro (DESIGN 2026).

From a literature review we built a validated dataset of 72 documented repurposing cases. Each model received the same zero-shot prompts asking for ten repurposing scenarios and the properties needed to realise them, and I helped set up these experiments (3,120 runs). The answers were matched against the documented cases and checked manually.

Spec Extraction by Distillation

Large language models read specification pages of product manuals well, but they are expensive in resources and money when many manuals have to be evaluated. Can a small model learn the same job from them?

gpt-5.6-terra labels manual pages as JSON grouped by physical component, or as empty for pages without specifications. On these labels we fine-tune the small vision-language models Qwen3-VL 2B and 4B with LoRA, using 80 % specification pages and 20 % empty pages so that they also learn what to ignore. Held-out manuals test whether the students return valid JSON, recognise empty pages and find the right components and fields.

Reinforcement Learning

Exploration vs. Exploitation

A reinforcement learning agent must balance exploring new options with exploiting what it knows. Which exploration strategy works best in mazes?

We compared constant and annealed ε-greedy, Count Based exploration and UCB on a DQN agent. The test bed is our own Gymnasium maze generator with 10×10 and 20×20 mazes, several seeds, and dense versus sparse rewards, with all runs tracked in Weights & Biases.

Graph Database

MaLeNa

Re-using parts of end-of-life products starts with knowing which devices share the same parts. In MaLeNa, I built the data basis: a graph database of phone specifications.

A scraper reads the phonedb.net datasheets and stores each phone as a Neo4j graph of components and their properties. A final merge step collapses identical components into one shared node, so a single Cypher query shows which phones share a display or battery.
